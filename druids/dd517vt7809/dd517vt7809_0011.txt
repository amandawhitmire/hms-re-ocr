The summation of the voltages during a spike in Na*-free and a spike
in Ca2+-free solutions does not result in the shape of a "normal" action
potential in ASW. This indicates that another factor is necessary for
achieving action potential shape. This factor may be the negative or
positive feedback that the currents experience as a result of the change in
potential. As the voltage increases, the probability or a Ca2+ or Na+
channel being open also increases. Adding the spikes observed in Na+-free
and Ca2+-free solutions increases the voltage which in turn changes Na+
and Ca2+ currents. To obtain a normal action potential shape by the
addition of Na+ and Ca2+ currents, these currents should be measured at
voltages ranging from resting potential to maximum spike height. As the
Na+-free curves and the Ca2+-free curves are added to form a higher

decreased the rate of hyperpolarization during an action potential as
would be expected if K+ current was hindered. The slowed
hyperpolarization that remained was probably caused by the inactivation
of Na+ and Ca2+ current. The undershoot at the end of an action potential
was abolished. This also was expected since this is caused by increased.

expected and does not seem to be a result of the direct action of the K+
