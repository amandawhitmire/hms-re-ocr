Discussion

It has been observed that in molluscan cell soma, the major currents
involved during action potentials are carried by Na*, Ca2+, and K+ ions (6).
Na+ and Ca2+ currents are necessary for the rising phase as Na* and Ca2+
ions enter the cell and K+ ions leaving the cell account for the subsequent
hyperpolarization during the potential. The experiments in which Na*-
free, Ca2+-free, and TEA sea water were used to observe action potentials.
in the selective absence of these ions seems to verify this. In all cases,
action potential shape was changed drastically. In Nat-free solution, the
sine wave like undulations indicate that Ca2+ current has a slower

activation and a slower deactivation than Na+ current. The repetitions of
the undulations seems to indicate that in these cells, Ca2+ current may be
necessary for repetitive firing. In contrast, with the Ca2+ free solution,
Na+ current turned on rapidly and deactivated rapidly. The difference in
the time it takes from the peak of the action potential to the
repolarization is different. Since K+ current should be the same in both
cases, this difference can be accounted for by the rapid deactivation of the
Na+ channels. In Ca2+-free solution, there are one or two sharp action.
potentials followed by a flat line. This seems to indicate that Na* current
by itself cannot maintain repetitive firing. A very simplified model of an
action potential would be that the Na+ current plays a major role in the
quick upward and downward slopes of the spikes while Ca2+ current plays
a major role in the repetition of the spikes.
