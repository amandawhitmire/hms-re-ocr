When the TEA solution and Ca2+-free Co²+ solution were mixed, an
interesting effect was observed in beating cells, shown in Figure 5.
Following a spike, a cell never hyperpolarized back to resting potential but
hyperpolarized to a voltage approximately 40 mV above rest. A very weak
second spike was observed followed by a several second flat interval
during which the voltage was slowly but consistently decreasing. After
reaching a certain voltage, slight action potential like spikes began
appearing growing in size (both action potential peak voltage was rising
and lowest voltage was becoming more negative). After these potentials
reached a certain height, voltage returned to resting potential. This
process was repeated in a beater like fashion. At resting potential, a
definite membrane resistance could be measured but during the time that
the cell was at the 40 mV above the resting stage, membrane resistance,
as measured by injecting a hyperpolarizing current, decreased to the
extent that a 1 nA injected current had an immeasurable effect on voltage.
The addition of ASW to the TEA/Ca2+-free Co²+ solution decreased the
amount of time spent in the higher voltage state. It could also abolish the
higher voltage state totally and instead, make the cell fire repetitively for
a short interval followed by an interval where the cell did not fire
spontaneously similar to a bursting pacemaker cell.
