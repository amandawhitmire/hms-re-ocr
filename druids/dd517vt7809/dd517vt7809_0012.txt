The addition of the combination of TEA solution and Ca2+-free Co²+
solution produced an interesting effect in beating cells. The cell seems to
be in an unstable equilibrium between two states: the resting state and a
state approximately 40 mV above the resting state. The resting state can
be explained by the permeability of the membrane to potassium ions.
Although TEA blocks some K+ channels, many K+ channels necessary for the
maintenance of the resting potential are not effected by TEA. Also, in the
combination solution used in this case, the TEA has been diluted by Ca2+-
free and ASW solutions. The state in which the cell is at a potential 40
mV above resting can be explained by the opening of Ca2+ channels. This is
unexpected since the presence of Ca2+-free Co would be expected to block
Ca2+ channels. However, the fact that the resistance of the cell at the 40
mV above resting state is very low compared to the resistance at resting
shows that this effect cannot be caused solely by a decrease in K+
conductance. An increase in Na+ conductance would explain this
phenomenon except that voltage gated Na+ channels would be expected to
inactivate instead of staying open so long. Therefore, this second state
seems to be explained best by a state achieved by the equilibrium of the
conductances of both K+ channels and Ca2+ channels, both of which have
been decreased by TEA and Ca2+-free Co.
