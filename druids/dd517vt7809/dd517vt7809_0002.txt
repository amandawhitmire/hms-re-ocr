Introduction

It has been hypothesized that second messengers play a role in
changing rates of ion currents in neurons thus changing action potential
shape resulting in different amounts of transmitter released from
synaptic terminals. Klein and Kandel have suggested a model where CAMP
levels may control sensitization in Aplysia gill withdrawal response.
According to this model, serotonin binding to receptors activates
adenylate cyclase leading to an increase in CAMP levels in the presynaptic
terminal of the sensory neuron. CAMP activates a CAMP-dependent protein
kinase which phosphorylates a voltage sensitive K* channel, inactivating
it. The subsequent decrease in K+ current prolongs the time of

depolarization during action potentials, allowing more calcium ions to
enter with the result that more transmitter is released from the terminal
(1). Castellucci et al. (2) and Kaczmarek et al. (3) have injected the
catalytic subunit of bovine cAMP-dependent protein kinase into Aplysia
sensory neurons and bag cells respectively. Action potential height and
width were increased. Results seem to indicate an increase in Ca2+
current and a decrease in K+ current were responsible for this effect.

TPA (12-0-tetradecanoyl-phorbol-13 -acetate), a tumor promoting
phorbol ester, has also been reported to increase action potential height in
Aplysia bag cells. Presumably, it acts by mimicing the effects of
diazoglycerol which activates protein kinase C. Injection of purified
protein kinase C also increases action potential height by increasing Ca²+

current (4). Ca channels may be phosphorylated by this kinase thus
bringing more channels from an inactive to an active state.
