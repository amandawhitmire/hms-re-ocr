Conclusions

Although the results of this experiment are far from conclusive, it
seems likely, from the current literature, and from some results here, that
second messengers do have an effect on action potential shape, frequency,
or threshold. Neurons do not just passively transmit action potentials. If
this were the case, learning and memory would not occur. Neurons must
integrate and modify the information that is transmitted to them. Second
messengers, among other things, seem to play a role in this modification.
Using second messengers, models for simple forms of learning, such as the
Klein and Kandel model for Aplysia sensitization, can be made. Therefore,
the effects that second messengers have on neurons should be further
studied .
