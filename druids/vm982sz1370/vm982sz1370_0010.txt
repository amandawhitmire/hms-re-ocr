We attempted to maintain a normal distribution of sizes
in the population in order to determine whether or
not different sized animals grew at different rates.
In no case, for either T. funebralis or A. asmi
were we ablt to note growth which exceeded our ex-
perimental error. Although only eight T. funebralis

and three A. asmi remained and were measured for
the entire nine months, 75 T. funebralis and ten A.
asmi were kept for a period of five months (January
to May ). Assuming that all I. funebralis of approx-
imately the same size grow at the same rate, replacing.
the animals should not negate the validity of our
results. Thus, if growth measurable by our methods
was occuring in the population at large, we should have
been able to record it.

Because all of our data is negative in the
sense that we have not been able to record growth,
no measurements are included in this paper.

Three possible reasons exist that might explain
why no growth was noted for either animal. First,
both T. funebralis and A. asmi grow slowly; this would
indicate that survivors of both groups have extremely
long life spans. Second, one must realize that
measurements were made during only nine months of the

year. It is possible that both T. funebralis and A.
