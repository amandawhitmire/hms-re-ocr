Flash photolysis of caged IP3 abolishes wave propagation

The activation of the IP3 receptor through flash photolysis of caged IP3 does not lead to
wave propagation (Fig. 10). Following photolysis (time=0), the change in fluorescence occurs
throughout the cell indicating that a Ca2+ wave does not propagate. This result is consistent with
the working model of wave propagation that assumes the IP3 receptor is distributed throughout
the cell, and not localized to a particular area.

DISCUSSION

Ca indicators exhibit unique decay phases

The use of FFP-18 as a Ca2+ indicator is advantageous in allowing one to monitor changes

in [Ca2+] near intracellular membranes. However, its high affinity for Ca2+ causes it to reach
saturation well before the Ca2+ signal peaks during stimulation with bradykinin. In contrast, mag-
fura-2, a low-affinity Ca²* indicator, presents the advantage of displaying spectral shifts at high
Ca2+ concentrations, allowing one to better resolve peak intracellular [Ca2+]. The difference in
binding affinity between these two indicators leads to fluorescence decay patterns that are unique
to the indicator following neurotransmitter stimulation. The slow decay to resting levels observed
in a cell injected with FFP-18 and stimulated with bradykinin (Fig. 1) suggests that FFP-18
reaches saturation well before maximum concentrations of intracellular Ca2+ (2μM) are reached.
