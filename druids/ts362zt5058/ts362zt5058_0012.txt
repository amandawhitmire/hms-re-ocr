Cooperativity at the IP3 receptor

In this set of studies, the exact delay to Ca2+ release from storage pools could not be
resolved due to limitations in the fluorescence video imaging equipment. In particular, the 200ms
delay following photolysis before imaging is accounted for by the 100ms shutter speed as well as
delay associated with camera response following shutter reopening (see Materials and Methods).
To avoid such limitations, a rapid photodetector such as a photodiode or PMT needs to be
employed .

Research on the IP3 receptor suggests that the receptor exhibits the property of

cooperative binding [Meyer et al., 1988]. The theory states that the binding of one IP3 molecule
to the IP3 receptor facilitates the binding of other IP3 molecules. In order for the receptor to
activate and thus serve as a channel for Ca2+ release into the cytosol, 3 to 6 molecules of IP3 need
to be bound. The highly cooperative opening of Ca2+ channels by nanomolar concentrations of
IP3 enables cells to detect and amplify extremely small changes in the concentration of this
intracellular messenger in response to various stimuli.
