Two mechanisms underlying the active wave propagation can be postulated. If IP3 is
produced at the wave front, it can act to sustain propagation through activation of subsequent IP3
receptors down the length of the cell. Alternatively, if Ca2+ interacts with IP3 receptors as the
model suggests, it can invoke a Ca2+-induced Ca2+ release at the wave front to sustain propagation
through the cell.

Future investigation

It has been suggested that IP3 needs to reach sufficient threshold levels in order to activate
IP3 receptors and subsequently cause Ca2+ release into the cytosol [Parker, 1988]. In order to
measure and quantify this hypothetical IP3 threshold, continued experiments using flash photolysis
of lower concentrations of caged IP3 need to be conducted. Furthermore, it would be equally
beneficial to stimulate cells with neurotransmitter, uncage IP3 at varying intervals during the latent
period, and measure changes in latency to Ca2+ release. Such studies could potentially determine
if it is [IP3] itself which regulates and determines latency. In addition, other steps in the
phosphoinositide signaling pathway merit further investigation to determine their roles in the

latency to Ca2+ release following neurotransmitter stimulation. Essential steps to investigate are
PIP2 hydrolysis, PLCẞ1 activation, and Gq/11 turnover.
