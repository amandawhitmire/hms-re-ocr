FIGURE 1: Calcium signal in response to bradykinin with high-affinity indicator. Example of
the dynamics of Ca2+ release from a cell injected with FFP-18, K* salt (1mM micropipette
concentration) and stimulated with bradykinin (100nM). Agonist is delivered at time=0, followed
by a 10-11 second latent period in which [Ca2+]; do not appreciably change. The latency is
followed by a 20 fold increase in [Ca2+]; over a 2-3 second time period. Intracellular [Ca2+] then
exhibits a slow decay, presumably toward resting levels.

FIGURE 2: Calcium signal in response to bradykinin with low-affinity indicator. Example of
the dynamics of Ca2+ release from a cell loaded with mag-fura-2/AM (5μM) and stimulated with
bradykinin (100nM). Following stimulation (time=0), [Ca2+]; do not appreciably change for a
period of 6-7 seconds. The latent period is followed by a 25 fold increase in cytosolic [Ca2+] over
a time interval of 2-3 seconds in which peak Ca²+ levels are resolved. Following attainment of
peak [Ca2+]i, a rapid decay to resting levels is observed.

FIGURE 3: Calcium signal in response to flash photolysis of caged IP 3. Cells injected with
caged IP3 plus fluo-3 (1mM micropipette concentrations) exhibit an instantaneous increase in
[Ca2+] following flash photolysis. The flash used to uncage IP3 is represented by the sudden
decrease in fluorescence caused by shutter closing. Cytosolic [Ca2+] begins to rise within 200ms
following exposure to the flash, and attain peak levels within 1 second following flash.
