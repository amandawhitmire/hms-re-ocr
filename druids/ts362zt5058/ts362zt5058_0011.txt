Flash photolysis of caged IP3 eliminates latency

Stimulation of neuronal cells with the neurotransmitter bradykinin results in a latent period
as long as 30 seconds in duration prior to release of Ca2+ from IP3 receptor-regulated Ca²+ storage
compartments in the ER (Fig. 1-2). By direct release of IP3 into the cytosol using flash
photolysis, this latency is shown to be eliminated (Fig. 3-4). Within 200ms of photolysis,
intracellular [Ca2+] begins to increase, reaching peak Ca2+ levels within 1-2 seconds, following
which levels decay exponentially. The time needed to reach half decay was measured to be 4.06s.
This decay time most likely correlates to the degradation time of IP3. This compares favorably
with biochemical measure of IP3 degradation of approximately 9 seconds [Wang et al., 1995]
Prior to this half decay point, it is likely that several IP3 receptors are still being activated by IP3
molecules remaining in the cytosol. Past the half decay point, it is postulated that all IP3 released
by flash photolysis has degraded, IP3 receptors are inactive, and Ca2+ levels slowly return to
resting levels.
