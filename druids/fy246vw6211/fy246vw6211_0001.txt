Six species of Pagurus, P. samuelis (Stimpson, 1857),
P. hirsutiusculus (Dana, 1857), P. granosimanus (Stimpson,
1859), P. hemphillii (Benedict, 1892), P. ochotensis (Benedict,
1851) and P. beringanus (Benedict, 1392) occur along the
shores by the Hopkins Marine Station, Pacific Grove, California.
The first four are found in the intertidal with more or
less, overlapping distributions. Although excellent keys
exist, notably that of Schmitt (1921), for distinguishing
among the adults, little information is available on the
larvae and juveniles of these species. Through the work
of Thompson (1903), Hays (1939), and Coffin (1950), the
larvae and immature adults of P. samuelis can be identified;
However the larvae and juveniles of the other species are
not recognizable on the basis of published accounts. For
the present work an attempt has been made to determine means
for identifying the larvae of the first five aforementioned
species and the young adults of the first four.

Methods

Collections were made throughout the intertidal region
at Hopkins Marine Station to obtain gravid females and
Immature adults of the first four species named. One gravid
female of P. ochotensis was obtained by diving at a depth
of about eight meters; no immature adults were found. No
samples were obtained of P. beringanus.
