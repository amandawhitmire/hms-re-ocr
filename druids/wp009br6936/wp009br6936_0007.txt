Neuronal viability was tested via low-light video
microscopy. When loaded with fura-2-AM followed by ionomycin and
manganese, a substantial increase in cellular calcium influx occurred.
This change confirmed previous membrane impermeability to calcium,
and suggested cell viability. In addition, cells from other cultures were
similarly loaded with fluo-3-AM followed by KC1. After KC1 addition, an
immediate cellular calcium influx occurred, which indicated that the
cells had been depolarized and were viable.

Control cultures were studied for longevity (see Fig. 1).
After 5 hours in culture, 85% of plated cells were undamaged, according
to trypan blue tests. This viability percentage decreased somewhat until
Day 5, at which time only 50% of the cells were still alive.

In a series of experiments, cultures of various ages were
exposed to 1mM glutamate for 5 minutes, stained with trypan blue the
following day (see Fig. 1), and examined under phase contrast. Although
no immediate visible change was apparent during the glutamate
exposure, latent effects of neuronal degeneration resulting in death were
seen within 24 hours. After this lapse of time, neurons in some plates.
were replaced by debris (See Fig. ?) and stained with 0.4% trypan blue. In
addition, only a fraction of the original culture remained plated 1 day
after exposure; a considerable number of cells had become detached
during the process of degeneration. Cultures not exposed to glutamate
remained completely plated. Death rates of cells which had remained
plated after 1mM glutamate exposure were as high as 68% over control
