However, based on the preliminary 2,0 data (shown) and other preliminary 2,0
experiments (data not shown), it seems that Ca2+ alone does have a negative effect on Zn2+
permeation. We speculate that intracellular Ca2+ may promote PLC activity and resultant
TRPM7 inhibition via PIP2 hydrolysis (Runnels et al., 2002), or that extracellular Ca2+ may
significantly interfere with zinc binding to TRPM7 at 100 μM Zn2+. Besides collecting more
meaningful data from better 2,0 experiments, in future 0,2 and 0,0 tests we will also loaded cells
with BAPTA AM, a strong Ca2+ chelator. Comparing a non-BAPTA 0,2 experiment with a
BAPTA 0,2 experiment will further elucidate calcium's effects on Zn²+ TRPM7 permeation,
since even in 0 mM Ca²+ there is some intracellular Ca²+.

Also, the only missing piece of information necessary to calculate the actual ion
concentration at any given time is the FMIN. This value will be determined by treating cells with
TPEN (Sensi et al., 1997). Finally, since the [Zn2+] we used was 100 μM, and since
physiological levels are closer to 10 μM (Perveen et al., 2002), it will also be important to
examine 10 μM Zn2+ permeation.

Conclusions

This study shows that cells overexpressing TRPM7 load Zn2+ more than cells with normal
expression of TRPM7, supporting the claim that TRPM7 is the dominant pathway for Zn²+
movement into cells. This study also shows that Ca2+ and Mg2+ inhibit Zn2+ movement through
TRPM7. Further experiments will be necessary to determine just how effective Ca2+ and Mg2+
