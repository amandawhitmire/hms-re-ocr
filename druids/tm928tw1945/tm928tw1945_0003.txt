Materials and Methods

Prionitis lanceolata was the alga selected for study because it's
associated microfauna showed the most marked effects near the outfall.
Ten stations (areas) were sampled around Pt. #2 with three replicates made
at each area (Fig. 1). The samples were analyzed in themanner previously
described (Clearman, 1970). Again, the Simpson and Shannon-Weiner indices

were used to compute values for species diversity.

At Pt. Pinos four key areas in the vicinity of the outfall (areas 4, 5, 6

and 7) and areas at the end of the point (area 10) and directly across
from the outfall (area 12) were resampled (Fig. 3, Clearman, 1970). Sample
analysis was carried out as before (Clearman, 1970).

The results of the original study, the summer resamples and the samples
at Pt. %232 were compared in terms of numbers of species present and the diversity

indices .

In order to statistically compare areas felt to be polluted with natural,
unpolluted areas, the data was divided into three groups. Pt. Pinos was divided
into two groups designated "polluted", composed of areas felt to be heavily
damaged, as shown by low values for the diversity indices and the small number
of species found, and "unpolluted", consisting of the remaining areas around
the point. These two groups and Pt. #22 (all areas) were compared by employing
the Student's t test, using the mean values and standard deviations of the
species diversity indices and the number of species of the three groups.

Finally, the "unpolluted" group at Pt. Pinos and the areas of Pt. %22 were
randomly divided into equal groups and compared by the Student's t test, as
above, to test the internal variability of the three criteria tested.
