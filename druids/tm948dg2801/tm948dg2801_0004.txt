In order to have some way of subjectively judging how well
the cuttlefish matched their background, the pictures of the
cuttlefish in the experimental tank were shown to 12 human

'judges' who were asked to rate the cuttlefish"

camouflage

performance on a scale of 0 (a bad match) to 3 (a good match).

Body patterns of

of the animals were

classified according to

the system developed by Hanlon (1986). The five patterns shown

by the animals were:

(1) Uniform light (see figure 2); to an overall light tone with
very few of no dark chromatophores expanded.

(2) Stipple (see figure 4): an overall uniform tone that is
slightly darker than #1 due to expansion of dark chromatophores.
(3) Weak disruptive (see figure 6): a wide variety of patterns,
with the common characteristic that the body is broken into

portions by transverse or longitudinal

longitudinal elements.

(4) Weak Zebra (see figure 5): a low contrast striped pattern

also called 'sandy-stripe' by Holmes (1940).

(5) Uniform Dark (see figure 3): expansion of all dark

chromatophores .
