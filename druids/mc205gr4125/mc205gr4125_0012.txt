In the second experiment of the series, the Mg2+ block was removed and Ca²+ was
better able to pass through TRPM7 channels. Values of Ffinal were seen to increase in a
Mg2+ concentration dependent manner. A percent block graph was designed and the half-
block or possible Kd was determined to be 500 μM Mg (fig. 8b). Control trials reflected a
similar increasing trend on a smaller scale. Some deviation from the trend may be
explained by experimental error. If this deviation appears consistently, however, it may
be an indication of more complicated ion-ion interactions at the binding site. It is also
notable that trials involving Ca²* influx appear to show more inconsistency between cells
than trials involving Ca2+ efflux (fig. 9). This inconsistency may be due to variability in
the cell cycle and the mechanisms that regulate internal stores and intracellular
concentrations of Ca2+ and Mg2+.

2+

In the third experiment of the series, in which various extracellular concentrations
of Mg2+ were held constant while Ca2+ was removed, results showed a sharp decrease in
fluorescence. In addition, the amount of change in fluorescence decreased as the
concentration of Mg2+ was increased. That is to say, higher Mg2+ concentrations meant
more blocking and less intracellular Ca2+ available for efflux through TRPM7 at the start
of the experiment. Again, Ca2+ flux was found to be dependent on the concentration of
extracellular Mg2+.
