The fast twitch fibers respond to direct repetitive stimulation
of up to 200 Hz (3-20 v/cm, 0.75 ms duration) with tetant of increasing
amplitude (see fig. 4a). High gain oscilloscope traces of these

tetani (not illustrated) show that the response is still not completely
fused at 200 Hz. Thus, the chart records in fig 4a appear inaccurately
fused due to the low frequency response of the chart recorder.

Indirect stimulation via nerve shocks produced fused tetani even

at quite low frquencies, as depicted in fig. 4b. At 600 Hz, maintained
contraction fails. The reason for this is unknown.

The fast fibers display typical, strong, extended contractures
in response to K-glutamate (134 mm) (see table 1), caffeine (20 mm)
and acetylcholine (3.5 mm). Records are not included in this paper.
A high potassium solution depolarizes the membrane, thus causing

contraction. Caffeine induces release of intracellular calcium reserves.

ACh is the presumed neuromuscular transmitter.
