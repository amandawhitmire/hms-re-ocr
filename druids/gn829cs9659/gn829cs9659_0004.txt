Simultaneous recording of junction potential and isometric tension:

A suction electrode on a motor nerve and an intracellular

microelectrode were added to the apparatus described above to record

neuromuscular transmission. The suction electrode had a

200,μ diameter

tip made of polyethylene tubing mounted on a glass capillary. Fibers
which were seen to contract in response to nerve stimulation were
impaled with the microelectrode close to the spine end of the muscle.
Membrane potential was measured as the voltage difference between the
intracellular microelectrode (30-80 megohms, filled with 3 m KC1) and
a Ag: AgCl bath electrode, taken through a WPI electrometer.
pulses were applied to the nerve using a WPI digital pulse generator.
Experiments ended with between 0-5 mV electrode drift.
Experiments utilizing two intracellular microelectrodes:

Stimulating

Fibers were impa led with two microelectrodes (20-30 m). One was
used for injecting current (filled with 2 m KCitrate) while membrane
potential was measured as described above. Injected current was
collected by a Ag: AgCl bath electrode and an operational amplifier,
current-voltage converter (virtual ground) circuit. Pulses were
generated, and recorded data were sampled by a digital computer-based
system described in Gilly & Scheuer (1984). The voltage clamp circuit
used is also described in that paper.
