INTRODUCTION

While certain types of vertebrate muscle have been intensely
studied, relatively little work has been conducted on fish muscle.

Work done show several distinct peculiarities of fish muscle compared
to typical vertebrate muscle (e.g., amphibian, mammalian). Higher
teleost muscle (including the sand dab's family, Acanthopterygi)
utilizes an unusual type of fast fiber innervation. Each fast fiber
is multiply innervated (Barets, 1961; Hudson, 1969), not focally like
most other vertebrate fast muscle. This type of fast muscle innervation

Fish muscle is claimed further atypical for vertebrates because fish

muscles studied thus far lack sensory organs known as muscle spindles.
Only a handful of electrophysiologic studies have examined fish

fast fibers. Most studies have found that muscle action potentials

are characterized by overshoots close to 0 mV or by failure to overshoot.
Spikes typically appear as graded regenerative responses and are not

all-or-none (Hagiwara & Takahashi, 1967; Hidaka & Toida, 1969; Hidaka & Kuriyama,

and Barets, 1961). Other vertebrate fast fibers do show overshooting,
all-or-nothing action potentials. Additionally, few studies have
investigated properties of isometric contraction in these fast fibers.
Of a limited sampling of muscles, seahorse fin muscle is reported as

one of the fastest, with an isometric contraction time as short as
10 ms to peak, and shows a fusion frequency of 120 Hz (Bone, 1978).
