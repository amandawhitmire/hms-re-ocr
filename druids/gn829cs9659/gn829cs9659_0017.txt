Figure 2. Fin musculature. 2a) The in situ orientation of the
myotome-inserting muscles is depicted (see arrow). 2b) Myotome-
inserting muscles (skin-inserting muscles removed). The arrangements
of the bones and muscles are illustrated. Note the orientation of the

Figure 3. Fast contractions. 3a) Traces of isometric tension
elicited by shocks of increasing strength as recorded on the oscilto-
scope. The top (horizontal) trace depicts baseline of shock strength
and the blips on the left indicate stimulus strength measured in volts.
The bottom shows isometric tension measured in mg. Note that peak
contraction occurs in 10 ms. 3 b) Isometric tension recorded from
a muscle before and after the short fibers are excised. 3c) The
chart records that correspond to the oscilloscope traces of 3b.
top record monitors the stimulus, while the bottom record monitors
tension. Single shocks elicited the twitches shown. Note that the
muscle was stretched after cutting, and this may have resulted in
recordings of falsely greater tension in the "long fibers only" record.

Figures 5 and 6. The top trace monitors the current that is passed

Figure 5. End-plate potentials. 5a) Stimulation through nerve.
Junction potentials in response to shocks are illustrated.

5b) Several junction potentials are recorded. Note the absence of
contraction even though threshold is reached, and note the discrepancies
in rise time of the various e.p.p.5. 5c) Stimulation via injecting

Figure 6. E.P.P.s and contraction: Contraction amplitude varies
with the number and frequency of stimuli. Tension output is sharply
dependent on the number (four in 6a vs. twelve in 6b) and frequency
(200 Hz in 6b vs. 400 Hz in 6c) of administered shocks. Note again
that e.p.p.s hold at a steady level, failing to depolarize further.
