Neuromuscular Transmission:

At most of the myoneural junctions in fast vertebrate muscle,
each nerve impulse is followed by a similar impulse in the muscle
fibers which propogates rapidly in both directions toward the tendons,
thus ensuring a sufficiently synchronous contraction along the length
of the fiber. While a synchronous contraction clearly can occur
in sand dab fast fibers, muscle impulses similar to nerve impulses
(presumably action potentials) are not seen. Only depolarizing
end-plate potentials can be driven. The junction potentials that
are elicited in response to repetitive nerve shocks summate temporally
to depolarize the muscle cell to about -40 mV and hold voltage at
that steady level, failing to depolarize further.
