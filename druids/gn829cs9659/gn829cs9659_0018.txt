Figure 7.

Fast fibers do not produce action potentials. This
recording of membrane potential was taken using two intracellular
microelectrodes. The top trace in the record indicates the injected
current in nA and the current pulses are seen on the left. This
trace also notes the 0 mV level. The bottom trace monitors membrane
potential. Shocks which brought membrane potential to or beyond
threshold for contraction (about -44 mV) caused twitches. Tension
is not recorded in this experiment, but contraction was seen through
the microscope. It is important to observe that while indirect
nerve shocks were unable to drive membrane potential more positive
than - 40 mV direct injection of current can take membrane
potential at least to a suprathreshold level of -20 mV. Under
such suprathreshold conditions, action potentials are still not
produced .

Effects of O Na, TTX X 0 Ca on contraction. Isometric

effect on tetanic contractions while Ca-free/EGTA media drastically
decrease tension output.

Figure 9. Independence of contraction from extracellular calcium.
A voltage clamp analysis demonstrates that extracellular calcium is
not required for fast fiber contraction. The points graphed are
numbered in order of successive determinations at the various holding
potentials. The average value for contraction threshold from e. p. p.
is -44 mV and is drawn on the graph (dotted line).
