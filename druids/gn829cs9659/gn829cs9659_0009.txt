That contraction will not be produced by a single or a few

junction potentials is demonstrated in figs. 5a, 5b 5c, and 6a.
Multiple (at least 5-6) junction potentials must sum in time to drive
membrane potential beyond threshold to cause contraction (fig. 6b, 6c).

While end-plate potentials fail to drive membrane potential more

positive than about -40 mV,

a

muscle cell can be depolarized further
using a two microelectrode protocol (see Methods). Current pulses
are injected to obtain suprathreshold (for contraction) shocks, and
the presence or absence of action potentials can be observed. As
demonstrated in fig. 7, action potentials are not produced in the

fast fibers. This constitutes fairly conclusive evidence that fast

fibers do not contract via an action potential propogating mechanism.
A rough estimate of input resistance can be calculated from

figure 7 if the entire fiber is treated as a behemoth resistor. A
value of about 50 Kohms is determined: this is considerably lower than
for a frog twitch fiber of approximately 100 Kohms (Katz, 1966).
Input resistance is directly proportional to junction potential
amplitude, and is an indicator of membrane resistance. A Low R
input

value implies a leaky resting membrane.
