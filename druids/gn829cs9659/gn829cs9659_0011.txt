Threshold for contraction is reached via graded membrane

depolarizations. An all-or-none mechanism is not in operation in
these fibers and action potentials do not propogate.

Thus this

system of local, graded depolarizations is capable of producing
synchronous contraction along the length of the fibers (just like
usual vertebrate twitch muscles) if they indeed are multiply
innervated as Bone (1978) suggests. Under this mechanism, usual
all-or-none action potentials would be unnecessary.

At least five junction potentials in fairly rapid succession
appear to be necessary to activate contraction, though the exact
number and frequency remain unresolved at completion of this project.
