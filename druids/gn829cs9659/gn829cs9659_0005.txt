RESULTS

Anatomy :

The fast fin muscle fibers used for study are located along the
posterior end of the body, from the regions of dorsal fins that are
elevated during the rheotaxtic response (Ueland, 1984) (see fig. 1).
Two groups of fin muscles exist with different origins: 1) from bones
between the ocular and non-ocular myotome masses (see fig. 2a, arrow)
and 2) from the skin (see fig. 2a). The fin spine is the common
insertion of both types. Only the first type of muscle was studied
in the present report. As shown in fig. 2b, one fin spine is moved
by a total of four myotome-inserting muscles. Of these four muscles,
an individual bundle from the ocular side was isolated for experimentation.

The arrangement of fibers within a single bundle is diagrammed

in fig. 2b (fiber arrangement sketch). The fast fibers are long and
run from a spine tendon to a tendon underneath the myotome mass (see
arrow in fig. 2b). The superficial long fibers are spindle -shaped
