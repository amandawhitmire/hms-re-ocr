63 corallimorphs observed at C4-C5 had crabs in association; 41 had one

corallimorphs at North wall were associated with crabs, and 100% of the

Figure 3 shows that 63.3% of the corallimorphs viewed had one crab
visible, 17.8% had 2 crabs visible, and 18.9% had no crabs visible. Thus,
overall 81.1% of the ninety corallimorphs viewed closely had crabs in

association .

Chorilia were seen with other coelentarate hosts. At C4-C5, three
crabs were seen under cerianthids, two under duster anemones (Liponema),
two under Anthomastus, and one in a shaggy sponge. At Point Joe, one
Chorilia was seen in association with a cerianthid and another was seen in
a folded sponge. One Chorilia at Anthomastus ridge was seen with an
Anthomastus .

Reproductive Activity of the Corallimorph

Corallimorphs were seen in strings of graded sizes suggesting they
might result from asexual reproduction. On footage at time code
10/26/89/11, 03:29:45:00, a corallimorph of about 5 inches in diameter
appeared to be dividing in half- the split occured at the mouth. Gilbert van
Dykheusen at the Monterey Bay Aquarium has some observation suggesting
incipient asexual reproduction in captive anemones.

Footage from 05/06/91/07, 02:19.15:00, shows a corallimorph
releasing gametes or larvae out of its mouth. The epidermis of the
corallimorph is a transparent enough to observe the ripe gonads. The
gonads were seen in bands associated with the margins of mesentaries.
