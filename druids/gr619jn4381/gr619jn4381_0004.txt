To further investigate the relationship, six Chorilia and three
corallimorphs were observed at the Monterey Bay Aquarium's research
center. Two crabs and two corallimorphs were held for 35 days; one
anemone and four crabs were collected May 6, 1991, and were held for 22
days. The organisms were maintained in aquaria with recirculated water
at 4-9 degrees celcius. The sea tables were kept in the dark. They were
observed with a red filtered flash light or dim ambient light. The
anemones and crabs were fed frozen krill every other day during the
observations. The food was set on the face of the anemones and on the
rocks next to the crabs.

Three female crabs released larve while in captivity. Drawings of
the larvae were made using a camera lucida. Newly hatched larvae were
placed on the tentacles of the anemone to determine if the anemone stung
the larvae. A compound microscope was used to view larvae at 1000x in
search of the stinging nematocyst cells of the anemone.

A test to determine photreceptivity of the larvae was conducted on
the second, third, and fourth days after one of the female crabs began to
release her larvae. A five inch by six inch grid was drawn on the side of a
one gallon clear plastic tub. The female crab sat next to her anemone on a
rock, the larvae she had released were contained in this tub by a thin
filter. Light was shined with a flashlight onto one 1" square for one
minute After the minute, estimated counts were made of the number of
larvae in each of the thirty squares.
