Quantitative relationship of clot protein to total
blood protein

Changes in the soluble protein content of the
blood were analyzed as indicated in Fig. 3. An anal-
ysis of the first separation (Supernatant I and Sediment
I) indicates a total protein content of approximately
35 mg. protein/ ml. of which 19% to 27% was in the form
of clot protein formed during the collection of blood,
plus any proteinaceous substances and any cellular
components attached to the clot. Although repeated
microscopic examinations of the blood failed to reveal
discreet cellular elements, any cellular blood com-
ponents present in low concentrations would be contained
within Sediment I. A further breakdown of Sediment I
shows that 66% is in the form of an insoluble protein,
while the remaining proteins could be washed away by
either 10% EDTA or 3% NaCl. The clot formed in Sedi-
ment I could not be reversed by treatment with EDTA.
No differences in the solvent property of NaCl and
EDTA was observed. This treatment with EDTA correlates
with parallel experiments done on the reversibility
of washed Sediment II. In a similar treatment with
3 ml. of 3% NaCl and 3 ml. of 10% EDTA on a clot in-
duced by 0.2M calcium chloride, the values were found
