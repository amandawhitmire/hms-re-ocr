The iridocytes are found in the skin over the whole body of the squid,
and are arranged in donut-shaped splotches in the third dermal layer of
the dorsal mantle. Iridescence of iridocytes seems to be caused by the
organization of iridosomal platelets of high refractive index located on
the periphery of the cell and surrounded by infoldings of the
plasmalemma (Brocco and Cloney, 1980). These platelets are aligned on
edge, perpendicular to the integument of the squid (Schäfer, 1938;
Kawaguti and Ohgishi, 1962; Mirow, 1972).

Iridocytes differ from reflector cells not only in the mechanism.
by which they produce structural iridescence, but also in their ability to
change their iridescent properties. The neurotransmitter acetylcholine
(ACh) causes a change in the strength of iridescence (Cooper and Hanlon,
1986), although no one has yet successfully identified the innervation of
the iridocyte dermal layer. This study was conducted to characterize the
nature of the receptor responsible for that effect, and the possible
signal transduction mechanisms that may help mediate the structural
changes involved in iridescence.
