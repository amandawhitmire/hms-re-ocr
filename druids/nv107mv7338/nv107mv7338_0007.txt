Slides collected on days 4 & 5 of the short term studies and
those collected for long term effects were concerned with counting
and identification of diatoms.

Stations 3 & 4, 20 and 30 meters distant from the outfall mouth,
generally show the greatest number of species and individuals adsorbed
to plates, (Graphs 10-13). Comparing nutrient data (Graph 14), with the
diatom counts shows that the stations with the most diatoms also appear
to be most enriched by PO or NO. These stations are also charac-
terized by the least tidal fluctuation. Station 6 showed what is
believed to be a "normal" growth pattern.

The genera Navicula, Licmophora, Fragillaria and Nitzschia are
frequent inhabitants of intertidal waters and dense populations are
often indicative of organic pollution, (Golubic, 1970). Their
occurrence andhigh numbers at Stations 3 & 4 and on rocks in an
adjacent tide pool (Fig. 1-x), suggests the possibility of utilizing
them as quantatative as well as qualitative indicators of pollution
levels. The genus Navicula has been reported to live near outfalls and
Bildo (1970), has reported growth in various concentrations of sewage.
However, Baldo also found that the genera Nitzschia and Fragillaria
appear to be more susceptible to sewage than is Navicula. Thus the
ratios of these genera in the outfall area could aid in determining
the concentrations of sewage in specific areas.

The appearance of diatomaceous films on test slides also coincided
with the appearance of flagellates, ciliates, cyanophytes and an as
yet unidentified filamentous fungi.

I feel the natural succession of early substrate colonizers (as
seen on page 5), is perhaps being aborted at this stage in the immed-
iate outfall area. The significantly high, possibly toxic, detrital
cover and the low numbers of diatoms at Station 1 suggest this area
to be inhospitable for invertebrate larval settlement.
