points. In this study I examine the apparent structural
weakness of the muscle scar layer both by comparing the

forces required to break shells of one species at different
locations and by comparing the forces necessary to crush shells
of different species at the same point on the shell. To see if
high and low-spired limpets differed in fracture strength I
focused on species that varied in relative height (figure 1).
Another reason for focusing in particular on the muscle scar
layer was that most limpet shells vary in thickness along the
shell (figure 2A), with a thickened margin and apex and a
relatively thin muscle scar layer. One objective of my study
was to determine if this thinning-out of the shell near the scar

allowed the shell to fracture more easily at the scar,

especially in comparison to the thickened margin.
