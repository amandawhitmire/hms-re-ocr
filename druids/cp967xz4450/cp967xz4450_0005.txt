The horseshoe-shaped muscle scar (figure 2B) on the

inner surface of limpet shells results from the attachment of

the foot adductor muscles. Although Chapin (1966) found C
limatule shells to break more easily along the length than
width of the scar, I was interested to discover if some feature
of the actual shell structure along the horeshoe-shaped scar
accounted for the fracture characteristics. In particular,
MacClintock (1967) describes the myostracal (muscle scar)

layer of the shell as differing from the typical

crossed-lamellar structure of limpet shells (figure 3A). At the

scar the muscle attachment interferes with the usual

shell-laying process of the mantle. The resulting structure is
usually prismatic, and Currey (1974) describes this structure.
as stronger than crossed-lamellar (tensile strength >60MN/m²
for prisms). The prisms of the myostracum form the inner
surface of the shell (figure 3B). Figure 4 shows the effects of
