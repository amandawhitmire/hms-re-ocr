Still, it appears that a crab has no general advantage in

crushing a shell at any one location on the shell. Thus despite
the apparent thinning of the shell at the muscle scar region,
some other factor is necessary to make attacks at the scar

vulnerable to crabs.

In considering the dimensions of a limpet shell at both
the muscle scar and the margin, it is clear that the grasping
orientation where a crab would have to open a cheliped least is

at the width of the scar. The width of the scar was found to be

significantly less than the other three shell dimensions for
each of the four species, suggesting that even relatively small
crabs could still crush large limpets by grasping them at the
width of the scar. For example, a crab that could open a
cheliped to a maximum of 7.1 mm at the tip would still be able
grasp a 20mm long Callisella limatula shell along the width of
the scar. Moreover, a crab with a 20mm maximum opening that
