Coliform bacteria counts

Coliform bacteria, which occur in the human intestine, are widely used
as an indicator of sewage pollution; where they are encountered there is also
the possibility of disease organisms being present.

Counts were taken on two dates during the study period, following the
procedure in Standard Methods of Waste and Water Analysis. Three primary
fermentation tubes at each of three dilutions were used, and a confirmed test
was made using eosin methylene blue agar. The results are presented in fig-
ure 4. In general, these correspond well to the current and dilution patterns
discussed above, with the exception that an occasional high count may be found
at some distance away (see samples 3 and 11 in figure 4.). As these were al-
ways in shallow, protected areas, it would seem that bacteria (not necessarily
from the effluent) sometimes happen into a pocket providing especially favorable
growth conditions, giving counts that do not reflect the actual level of ef-
fluent dilution (but perhaps significant from the standpoint of public health).

Dissolved oxygen

Micro-organisms tend to attack and break down at a high rate the organic
matter discharged in sewage, using up in the process large quantities of

oxygen ,

which is vital to larger plants and animals. Thus, oxygen depletion

can be a measure of sewage pollution.

Dissolved oxygen determinations were done concurrently with the coli-
form counts, using the standard Winkler titration method.
