Further experimentation would also be required to determine whether
some of the other aspects of the results are true effects of compartmentation
of arginine or mere artifacts. No other strong inferences can be drawn from
these results because only two trials were run for each type of extract.
Although the agreement between all of the sets of trials was very good, more
trials need to be run to determine whether or not the variation between the
measured arginine levels is due to actual changes in compartmentalized and
cytoplasmic arginine or to experimental error. For instance, the levels of
compartmentalized arginine seem to rise after fertilization. This would
assume an uptake of free arginine from the cytoplasm to the compartments
or proteolysis of proteins contained within the compartments. The increase
however, is sufficiently small so that it cannot be considered significant over
such a small sample size.

A more significant feature, at least in magnitude, is what appears to
be a drop in free arginine levels after fertilization. After fertilization, the
level of cytoplasmic arginine per cell is .09 picomoles lower than before
fertilization. If this value proves to be significant, then it could result from
the increase in arginine phosphate which rises, at fertilization, to .14
