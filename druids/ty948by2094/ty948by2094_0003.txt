The second condition for a rise in the [AP]/[A] ratio to signal a similar
increase in [ATP]/[ADP] ratio is that the H+ ion concentration must remain
constant. However, at fertilization, the internal pH of the egg increases.
While in theory this increase in pH should cause a rise in AP/A ratio on its
own, Winkler and Linder found though that the rise in pH at fertilization is
not enough to account for the rise in arginine phosphate at fertilization.
They also found that a rise in arginine phosphate from artificial activation in
sodium free seawater in the presence of A23187 ionophore which has the
effect of activating the egg while keeping pH constant produces an increase
in arginine phosphate nearly equal to that observed in fertilized controls.
Assuming that arginine kinase is active both before and after
fertilization and assuming that the increase in pH at fertilization does not.
greatly affect the synthesis of arginine phosphate in vivo, the rise in the
[AP]/[A] ratio that occurs at fertilization should signal a similar rise in the
ratio of ATP to ADP. However, such an increase in the IATP]/[ADP] ratio is
not generally observed. There is little consensus as to what actually happens.
to the ATP/ADP ratio. For instance, Epel (1969) has observed that it remains
constant while Winkler and Linder measure a decrease in the ratio after

fertilization .
