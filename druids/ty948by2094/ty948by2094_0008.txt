As with most data, the data reported in this paper bring up far more

questions than they answer, requiring more experiments to be performed.
If nothing else, the data did grant some strong evidence for a hypothesis that
was completely overlooked by many in the field. Although more evidence is
needed to determine whether or not arginine is sequestered into slowly-
exchanging compartments in the sea urchin egg, experiments that could
obtain this evidence would be relatively simple to carry out. The
significance of the actual finding of arginine compartments would extend
beyond the study of egg activation. It could also lead to studies into the
channeling of amino acids, into the energy needs of the developing embryo
and perhaps even to differentiated cells.
