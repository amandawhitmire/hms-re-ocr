Discussion: These results give strong evidence that arginine is significantly
compartmentalized in the sea urchin egg. The values of total arginine per
cell of 50 and 47 picomoles of arginine per cell compare favorably with
results of Gross and Fry as repoted by Giudice (1973) of 32 picomoles of
arginine per cell. Some research tends to suggest that compartments of
arginine and other amino acids could exist in the sea urchin egg. Lee and
Epel (1983) found acidic granules in fertilized Strongylocentrus purpuratus
and Lytechinus pictus eggs which were stained by acridine orange. These
granules could conceivably accumulate basic amino acids such as arginine.
Ilan and Ilan (in press) discovered that when methionine is present in an
exogenous medium to 1 pictus eggs, it is incorporated into proteins at a
higher rate than intracellularly injected methionine. This result suggests
that amino acid channeling, a process which could require slowly-exchanging
compartmentation of amino acids occurs in the sea urchin egg.
