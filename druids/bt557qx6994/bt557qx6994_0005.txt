Considering the vital role of magnesium and possible links of magnesium

regulation in the TRPM7 channel, recent interests in the TRPM7 channel have been
shifted towards its possible role in magnesium homeostasis (Wolf, 2004). TRPM7 have
been shown to have a clear role in mediating magnesium uptake into the cells. The
native conductance of TRPM7 was previously identified in Jurkat T Lymphocytes and
RBL Cells, and described as the MagNuM (magnesium-nucleotide-inhibited metal) and
MIC (magnesium-inhibited cation) (Nadler et al., 2001; Hermosura et al., 2002, Prakriya
and Lewis, 2002). Cahalan and Kozak (2003) show that in addition to internal free Mg2+,
other divalent cations, including Ba 2+, Sr 2+, Zn2+, and Mn2+, also inhibited current
through the TRPM7 channel. But considering the higher Mg2+ concentration in the cell,
Mg2+ has a larger inhibitory effect. Monteilh-Zoller et al (2003) showed that although
TRPM7 is nonselective to metal ions, with affinities of Zn2+ Ni²+ >> Ba²* > Co²+ > Mg2+
≥ Mn2+ ≥ Sr²+ ≥ Cd2+≥ Ca2+, physiological conditions mainly permit the transport of Mg2+,
which is one of the main cations present in the extracellular fluid. This study attempts to
shed some light on the relation of TRPM7 and extracellular Mg 2+. By studying
interactions of extracellular Mg 2+ and Ca2+ through TRPM7 at physiological
concentrations, we are able to further understand how TRPM7 regulates these cations.
