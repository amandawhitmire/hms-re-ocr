Comparing significance of effects between Ca2+ influx in the presence of Mg2+,
Gd³+, and in non-induced cells

To assess whether the Mg2+ and Gd³+ is blocking other channels in the HEK293
cells, an ANOVA comparison was used to test significance of the maximum
fluorescence rise in the cells exposed to 2mM Ca/2mM Mg2+ (n=12), 2mM Ca2+ /20uM
Gd3+ with Gd3+ tested first in order (n=6), 2mM Ca2+ /20uM Gd3+ with Gd³+ tested
second in order (n=6), and the non-induced cells exposed to just 2mM Ca 2+ (n=24).
Results show no significant effects (p=0.5) when the two Gd³+ tests were pooled
together (n=12), indicating that Mg 2+ and Gd³+ perhaps only blocks TRPM7 channels.
However, the significance seen in cells that were exposed to Gd3+ in the second order
indicate that further tests will need to be done to analyze this effect.

DISCUSSION

Results from this study hold important implications for Ca 2+ and Mg 2+ signaling
through the TRPM7 channel. It is shown that the mTRPM7 gene, when overexpressed
in HEK293 cell line, is a Ca2+ channel that allows Ca2+ accumulation in Mg2+-free.
extracellular environment. Influx of Ca2+ was also present in non-induced cells, although
in relatively low amounts. This reflects the presence of other Ca2+ channels in the
HEK-293 cells, which also contribute to the increased fluorescence value seen in the
induced cells. However, there is a high significant difference between induced and non-
