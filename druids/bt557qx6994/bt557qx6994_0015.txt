for binding site within the channel. Thus, under physiological conditions, the channel is
more specifically targeted towards magnesium, as well as allowing an entry way for
trace metals, since TRPM7-mediated trace metal entry were also found to occur at
physiological levels of Ca 2+ and Mg2+ (Monteilh-Zoller et al, 2003). In addition,
considering that Gd3+ inhibits Ca2+ influx in a Mg2+-free solution, it would be interesting
to conduct further research on the possibility of a Gd³* block on Mg2+ influx under normal
physiological conditions.

Previous research shows Ca2+ influx is blocked by 0.6mM internal Mg2+, during
which channel allows efflux of monovalent ions. Therefore, it seems that both internal
and external Mg2+ levels act in a complementary manner to block Ca2+ influx. In
addition, Schmitz showed that millimolar concentrations of internal Mg 2+ completely
blocked the inward current through TRPM7, which also blocks Mg 2+ influx. The negative
feedback loop therefore allows homeostatic regulation of Mg2+ through TRPM7. The
importance of TRPM7 in regulation of Mg2+ influx is shown in knockout experiments
