We also demonstrated the inhibitory effects of both 2mM Mg2+ and 20 μM Gd³+
on Ca2+ influx through the TRPM7. Ca 2+ influx is blocked by Gd³+, which is a trivalent
cation that commonly blocks mechanoreceptors and Ca2+ channels. Considering that
Gd3+ has a similar ionic radius to Ca 2+ (0.938 Å as compared to 0.99 A, respectively),
Gd3+ perhaps serves as a cation competitor that mimics Ca2+ in its binding to the pore of
ion channels (Hamill and McBride, 1996). The finding that Gd³* shows a reversibility of
effect indicates that Gd³* does not permanently bind to the competing binding site in the
ion channel. The usage of Gd³* serves as a tool for characterizing the channels, and
for studying the permeability pathways. Our results open up a gateway for further
investigations into the mechanisms of the TRPM7 channel, which may hold broad
implications for the TRP family.

Similar to Gd3+, we propose that there may be binding site competition, in which
Mg2+ binds to a site in the channel where Ca2+ normally binds to. Our results show that
the presence of 2 mM Mg2+ significantly inhibits Ca2+ influx through the TRPM7 channel.
This is consistent with an electrophysiological study, where it was found that at above
micromolar concentrations of external Mg2+, the I/V shape of the monovalent current
through the channel was altered, and inward currents were preferentially blocked.
