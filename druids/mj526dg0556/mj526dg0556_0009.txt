Respiration

The normal respiratory rate for B. glandula was calculated

to be 180 μl 02/8/hr., while rates of 96 μl 02/g/hr. and 260 μl 02/8/hr.

were found for B. tintinnabulum and B. nubilis respectively.

After having been subjected to the deoxygenated water for eighteen

hours, the rate increased to 925 μl 02/g/hr. for B. glandula
while B. tintinnabulum only increased its rate to 400 μl 02/8/hr.
and B. nubilis to 310 jul 02/g/hr. In comparing the rate of resp-
iration in water to that out of water, it was found that B. glan-
dula decreases its rate with increasing exposure. The rate was
the same for animals that had been freshly removed from the sea-

water but after six hours of exposure declined to 92 μl 02/g/br.
and to 10 μl 02/8/hr. after thirty-six hours of dessication.

exposed to dessication is similar to that which Barnes and Barnes (1957)

lar opening which was observed in the latter species was absent
in B. glandula. The two lower intertidal species studied, B. nub-
ilis and B. tintinnabulum, however, exhibited cirral pulsing even
