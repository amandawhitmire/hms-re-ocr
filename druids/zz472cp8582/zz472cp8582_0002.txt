INTRODUCTION

The gastropod Calliostoma canaliculatum is one of many species
of Mollusca that exhibits an escape response when provoked by an
asteroid (Bullock, 1953; Feder, 1963, 1967; Montgomery, 1967; Mauzey
et al., 1968; Phillips, 1976; Dayton et al., 1977; Moitza and
Phillips, 1979; Abbott and Haderlie, 1980; Harrold, 1981, 1982;
Herrlinger, 1983; Watanabe, 1983). C. canaliculatum reacts
particularly violently to the seastar Pycnopodia helianthoides.
Many of the initial stages of this escape response are shared with
other snails of this genus (Harrold, 1981; Herrlinger, 1983), but
C. canaliculatum also secretes a noxious yellow substance from its
hypobranchial gland when stimulated by Pycnopodia (Baxter and Gilly,
personal communication).

This added feature to the typical

Calliostoma genus escape mechanism appears to be quite effective.
Herrlinger, 1983, reported that although gastropods made up 79% of
the typical Pycnopodia's diet, only about 1.5% of these were

C. canaliculatum compared to C. ligatum which makes up almost 25% of
the gastropods eaten. C. ligatum does not display the secretion of
the yellow substance.

The objectives of this study were to extensively observe the
stages of C. canaliculatum's escape mechanism from Pycnopodia, to th
extent that it became predictable, and to determine the source of th
noxious exudate as being derived from the snail's diet of the kelp
Macrocystis pyrifera, the red algae Gigartina corymbifera, and the
encrusting invertebrates present on these plants (Hunt, 1977;
Sellers, 1977; Abbott and Haderlie, 1980) or from some endogenous

source .
