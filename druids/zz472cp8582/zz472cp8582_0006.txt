RESULTS :

Behavioral Experiments:

A predictable escape response was determined for C.
canaliculatum from Pycnopodia on both

horizontal and vertical

surfaces. On the horizontal substratum the following was

observed :

1) When a Pycnopodia tube foot was touched to the head
tentacles of C. canaliculatum, the snail quickly turned
away from the stimulated area (Fig. 2).

2) When "attacked" from behind C. canaliculatum lifts up its
shell and begins to run away. Approximately 50% of the
snails tested in this manner released the hypobranchial
secretion while they were running, and this tends to
stick to the snail's shell and mucus trail (Fig. 3).

3) After repeated stimulation from behind, the snail begins
to rotate its shell back and forth while throwing its foot
against the substratum causing a somersaulting reaction
away from the Pycnopodia. In 100% of animals tested, the
yellow secretion response takes place in conjuction
with this movement, wrapping around the snail as it
rotates (Fig. 4).

4) Once on its back the snail continues to rotate its foot
until it is stimulated again by the tube foot. At this
point the animal retracts most of the way into its shell,
gives a final pulse that shoots out more exudate, and
pulls all the way back into its shell (Fig. 5).
