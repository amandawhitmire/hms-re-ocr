But, the eighty original snails that were induced to secrete the

secretion rate, and not all of them secreted the "6" equivalent.
This indicates that some of these snails had probably been recently
attacked in the field and forced to use the exudate to escape and not
had time to regenerate it before they were captured. This has the
potential to allow predator-prey studies in free ranging populations
by measuring the numbers of attacks that were not successful.

Furthermore, when comparing the averages calculated for both
the amounts and the bioassay reactions (Figs. 10, 11), it appears
that four days is not enough time to generate the strong "6"
reading for the amount of secretion released, but it is a long
enough time to generate a strongly volatile secretion, therefore:

1) The recovery period necessary to produce the largest
amount of the exudate lies somewhere between the longest
tested period, 4 days, and the observed period at which
100% of the C. canaliculatum population secreted the
equivalent to a "6" on the scale, 7 days.

2) Since the "6" on the Pycnopodia reaction scale did not
correspond to the "6" on the scale for amount of yellow
observed, it appears that the active reagent of the
compound does not lie in the yellow part of the substance.
