INTRODUCTION

Pacific Grove, in Monterey County, California, is a
city of approximately 15,000 people which disposes of about
one and one-half million gallons of primary treated sewage
each day. The sewage outfall is located intertidally on
the rocks of Point Pinos, which demarcates the southern
extremity of Monterey Bay. The primary treated sewage,
which is subject to heavy chlorination, is discharged 800
feet from the base of Point Pinos in a rocky intertidal
area at a tidal height of about mean lower-low water (0.0 feet).
A preliminary examination of this area showed several inter-
tidal organisms to have distributions which appeared to
be affected by the presence of the nearby outfall. That
is, these organisms had very sparce distributions for as
much as 100 feet around the outfall pipe, but showed increased
abundance with increased distance from the outfall.
