In

the field, on the other hand, the concentrations of chlorine
observed are fairly constant because of the relatively
steady flow of fresh effluent. Wave action may be another
factor contributing to this discrepancy between laboratory
and field work. In the lab the only water movement the
anemones had to cope with was that brought about by gentle
aeration. Some constituent in the sewage could be weakening
the attachment apparatus, but this might be noticeable only
under the stress of continual water surge. Thus in the
field the effects of sewage effluent might not bring
about death, as it does in the laboratory, but rather it
might seriously interfere with the ability of the anemones
to remain attached to their substrate. In this case, the
detached anemones would not have very good chances for
survival .
