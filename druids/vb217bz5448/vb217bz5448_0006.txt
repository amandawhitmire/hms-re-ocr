During the course of this field study it was noticed
that in the large tidepool in area 2 the anemones were
covered with a peculiar sheath of mucous-like material.
Six weeks after the studies were initiated this same popu-
lation had progressed to a stage where individuals were
easy to pry off their substrates. They also appeared to
be in the process of everting their gastro-vascular cavities,
giving the anemones a bloated appearance. At the conclusion

of the study these anemones were all dead.

In order to more precisely relate the natural distribution
of the anemones to the direct effects of the sewage field,
granite boulders with approximately 15 anemones attached
were put out at six stations (see figure 5). Five of these
were positioned at stations 1,2,3,5, and 6, which had earlier
been used for chemical analysis, and the sixth was placed in
a tidepool on the north side of the point which, on the
basis of chemical analysis, appeared to be normal.
