A third point that came up during the investigation
was the possibility that the sewage was having an effect
on the algal symbionts, zooxanthellae, usually present in
Anthopleura elegantissima. The symbionts are known to

be ejected upon treatment of the host with various chemicals.
or heat (Buchsbaum, 1968). This ejection could be influencing
survival because zooxanthellae produce oxygen which could
make up for oxygen deficiencies in the polluted water.
Thus it was thought that the presence of zooxanthellae
could very well influence the distribution of anemones
in the study area. While populations of anemones around
stations 3 and 5 were found to be quite variable in respect
to whether or not they contained zooxanthellae, no correla-
tion with pollution could be made. Furthermore, anemones
that had been in high concentrations of Pacific Grove
sewage did not eject their zooxanthellae at any time.
