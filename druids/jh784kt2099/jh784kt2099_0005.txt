Materials and Methods

Sample collection and storage During the months of April and
May, 1990, I collected samples of Tetraclite rubescens from jetties
in Port Heuneme, Morro Bay, Moss Landing, and from a cement wall in
Pacific Grove (figure 1). Samples of Sernibalanus cariosus (Pallas,
1788), originally mistaken for Tetraclite, were collected from a jetty
in Half Moon Bay. In all locations samples were collected throughout
the species vertical intertidal range. In Pacific Grove the sampled
individuals ranged in size from very small (2 mm basal diameter) to
very large (50 mm basal diameter). in all other locations I took only
medium to large individuals. I removed the soft tissue from all but
the smallest individuals (4 mm basal diameter or less) with tweezers,
and stored each sample in an individual plastic centrifuge tube. The
smallest individuals were simply placed shell and all into individual
tubes. The samples from Half Moon Bay, Morro Bay, and Port Hueneme
were frozen in a cooler of dry ice immediately after sampling, and
then stored at -70 degrees C for the remainder of the project. The
