Results

Geographic variation:

Of the five scored loci, Pgi-1, Pgm, To, and Hk were polymorphic

in all populations studied. Mdh-2 was monomorphic in all populations.

studied. Table I shows the allele frequencies for all loci at each

Tetraclite study site. Alleles are referred to by letter, with A being
the fastest moving allele, and F the slowest. For Pgi, each sample site
has one very common allele (75-85%), one intermediate allele (5-15%)
and two or three rare alleles (<5%). All populations had the same
common allele, but they differed in the frequencies of the rarer alleles
(see figure 2). In Morro Bay, allele C is the second most common
alleie, while in all other populations allele B is the second most-

common. None of the Moss Landing samples showed allele E, which had
a frequency of 5% in Pacific Grove, only 20 miles away. The Port
Hueneme samples lacked allele A. The Pgi allele frequencies were
found to differ significantly between sampling locations using a G test
for independence (Sokal and Rothf, 1981.) All the Pgi heterozygotes
displayed a three banded pattern on the gels typical of a dimeric
enzyme. There were six alleles at the Pgm locus, and they fall into a
pattern similar to Pgi. All populations had one very common allele,
one intermediate allele, and several rare alleles (figure 3). The
differences in Pgm allele frequencies were not found to be significant
(p<.1). Tetrazolium oxidase was scored only for Morro Bay and Port
