Experimental

The surfaces from which the snails were selected are rep-
resentative of the position of the Littorina planaxis population
in the intertidal zone at Hopkins Marine Station, Cabrillo Point,
Pacific Grove, California. Snails came predominantly from surfaces
4-12 feet above mean low tide, and were sheltered from direct wave
action, receiving splash only at high tide. No distinction was
made as to the sex of the animals used in these studies.

The preparation of the enzyme extract from the digestive
gland involved collection, starvation and dissection of the snails
described above. The shells were broken in a small vice and the
right shell muscle detached from the shell remnants. The midguts
were removed posterior to the left kidney, and contained, in addi-
tion to the digestive diverticula, gonadal tissue, the stomach
and stomach contents. In snails weighing 1.0-1.5 g., this
complement weighed 30-50 mg., or 3-4% of the total weight. All
preparative procedures following the weighing were carried out
in an ice bath to retard denaturation of the enzymes. Sufficient
digestive gland to give 10-15 mg. of tissue or its equivalent
homogenate per milliliter of final reaction mixture was homogenized
in artificial sea water, ASW (MacLeod, et. al., 1954), with a
Thomas tissue grinder, (Cat. #4288-B, Philadelphia). Centrifugation.
for 30 minutes in a Serval centrifuge, Model SS-1, to precipitate
cell fragments, followed by filtration through Whatman #1 paper to
remove lipids, readied the tissue homogenate for use in the
reaction mixtures. Each reaction mixture contained 1 ml. of
1% casein, obtained from non-fat milk solids by the method of
Cohn (1930) and prepared according to Kunitz (1947) at pH 7.0.
