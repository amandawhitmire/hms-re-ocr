Observations were made to determine changes in quantity,
class composition by bulk, potential organic input from
drift, and the quantity of drift arriving on three specific
areas of the beach. Both this study and ZoBell's concur
(1) that) the quantity of drift deposited on the beach is
related to the amount of off-shore growth, and to water
and wind movements as indicated by wave heights; (ii)
that residence time of depositions of drift is a factor of
the high water levels which follow it; and (iii) that
slope of the beach changes with large depositions of sand
(also linked to wind and water movements), and this affects
the quantities of drift remaining on the beach.

The beach studied has a west north west exposure, and
is flanked by rocks north and south. It was approximately
75 meters by 20 meters at the beginning of the study.
Intertidally, as well as sub-tidally, thes rocks provide
a good base for many species of Rhodophyta, Phaeophyta,
surf grass (principally Phyllospadix), and a few species
of Chlorophyta. Offshore, to the north and south of the
cove, there are large kelp beds (Pearse, et. al., 1971),
consisting principally of Macrocystis pyrifera, Cystoseira
osmundacea, and Dictyoneuropsis reticulata (Pearse, et. al.,
1971). Winds, when present, prevail from the north west to
the east. The main current effecting the deposition of
drift on the beach is north-south, resulting in the majority
of drift and sand being left on the southern portion of
the beach, (Smith, 1968) (Figures 2,3).
