Nonetheless, many evolutionary advantages of the fibre system
have been suggested, namely that the system has better synchronicity
and allows the animal to exhibit a faster escape response. As
J. 2. Young wrote, "The value of systems of giant fibres to the
animals possessing them lies presumably either in the greater speed
of conduction in larger axons, or in that such large fibres make it
possible to call into action a large number of effector agents
nearly simultaneously" (J. 2. Young, 1938).

The work presented in this paper addresses both of these
presuppositions directly, by using the traditional, open-mantle
preparation to determine the limit of simultaneity, and by examining
the first obtained in vivo neural recordings from the stellar
nerves of Loligo Opalescens. Data collected suggests that the
former could be true, while the latter does not aid in allowing
the animal to escape faster. Furthermore, this work indicat
probable evolutionary pathway for the development of the giant

fibre system.
