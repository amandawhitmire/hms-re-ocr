I used lactate dehydrogenase-A (A4-LDH) to explore the mechanism by which
homologous proteins might adapt to different thermal environments. A4-LDH is
the enzyme that converts pyruvate and NADH to lactate and NAD+ during
anaerobic glycolysis. In order to describe temperature adaptation, I compared the
sequences and apparent Michaelis-Menten kinetic constants of pyruvate (Km-PYR)
among four elasmobranch A4-LDH orthologs. Km values are typically very sensitive
to temperature, usually rising as measurement temperature is increased. However,
in previous studies comparing various organisms' pyruvate Km's over a range of
temperatures, it has been found that enzymes tend to conserve Km at the
temperatures at which they function (Somero 1995, Holland et al. 1997). In this
study, changes in primary structure were compared with the different ways in which
the Km-PYR varied with temperature among the four species' LDH orthologs.
Knowledge of LDH 3-D structure (Abad-Zapatero et al. 1987) and conformational
changes during catalysis (Dunn et al. 1991) were used to interpret correlations
between kinetics and sequence variation, and to compare these correlations to
biochemical temperature adaptation in teleosts.
