The two most common species of Tegula in the Hopkins kelp forest
are 7 brunnes, which is most abundant from 0-6 m, and 7 pulligo, which is
abundant in deeper water. A third congener, T. montereyi is less common
but is found at depths similar to T pulligo (Lowry et al. 1974, Watanabe
1984b) Although the majority of the snails are found up on kelp plants, a
substantial number can also be found on the substrate. Densities of these
snails on the substrate has been quantified by Watanabe (1982) and Harrold
et al. (1988).

I used copper-painted patio stones as experimental Tegula -exclusion.
plots. Copper has been used effectively by a number of experimenters (eg.
Cubit 1975, Robles 1982, Johnson, in prep.), and recently a pilot study was
conducted at Hopkins Marine Station by James Leichter, demonstrating the
potential effectiveness of copper strips as barriers to Tegula spp. (J.
Leichter, 1989, personal communication.)

T. brunnea is very abundant on the substrate in the shallow subtidal.
Watanabe (1982) found mean densities of 37.6 +/- 3.8(SE) T. brunnea m-2
in this depth range. Kelp plant density at this depth is considerably lower
(pers. observ.). By placing experimental plots in the kelp forest at depths of
7.6 m and 2.4 m, I hoped to determine whether Tegula grazing pressure
varied with depth.
