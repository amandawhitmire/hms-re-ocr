field experiments

All field experiments were done in the kelp beds off of Hopkins
Marine Station of Stanford University in Pacific Grove, CA, USA (36°36'N,
121954 " W ) .

In the field, slides were held by surgical tubing attached to 25.4 cm X
15.2 cm X 0.6 cm Plexiglass sheets. The Plexiglass was attached with 2
masonry screws to circular concrete patio stones, 30.5 cm diameter and 5.1
cm thick. For each set of three treatments, I painted the entire outer
circumference of one patio stone with copper paint (Interlux Copper-Lux
Antifouling Bottom Paint, 66.5% cuprous oxide), left one bare, and painted
one in alternating 450 arcs of copper paint and bare concrete. Each patio
stone could hold up to 8 microscope slides. (Figure 2)

For the first set of experiments, 3 replicates of 3 patio stones each
were placed on the substrate in the Hopkins kelp forest at 7.6 m depth. Each
set of patio stones was within 2.5 m or less of an adult Macrocystis. All
three replicates were located within 13 m of one another, in the hope that
this would minimize between-site differences. 4 gametophyte slides (plated
at a density of 32.8 gametophytes mm-2) and 2 clean slides were placed on
each patio stone. The clean slides served as a control to determine any new
gametophyte recruitment that would have occurred during the experimental
period. Two gametophyte slides and one control slide were collected after 2
days then after 6 days in the field. New gametophytes (at a density of 59.2
gametophytes mm-2) were put out immediately following this experiment,
and they were collected after 5 and 7 days. Data from the two experiments
were combined for analysis.
