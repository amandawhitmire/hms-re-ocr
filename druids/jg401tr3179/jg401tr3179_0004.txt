were grown on frosted glass slides (Fisher fully frosted cytology slides) at
densities from 33 mm-2 to 180 mm-2. Glass slides were chosen as a
settling surface because they are easily manipulated and they facilitate
microscopic viewing of the gametophytes. Ripe sporophylls were collected.
from the kelp bed off of Hopkins Marine Station. Spore release was achieved
by cleaning 20 or so ripe sporophylls with a paper towel to remove any
epiphytes, and then soaking the sporophylls in chilled filtered seawater for
2-3 hours. The density of the resulting spore solution was measured using a
hemacytometer. Densities used for plating ran from 8.1 X 104 spores ml-1
to 5.9 X 105 spores mi-1. The solution was poured 3 to 4 cm high in a tray
holding frosted glass slides. The spores were allowed 24 hours to settle
before the trays were washed in fresh filtered sea water. After three days,
the gametophytes were large enough to clearly count and identify under a
45% objective of a compound microscope. Gametophyte survivorship was
measured by scanning 15 evenly-spaced 25 mm2 fields on each slide. Only
the middle inch of each slide was examined, leaving an inch on each side to
manipulate the slides in the field. All slides were kept submerged in
filtered sea water in a Plexiglass slide holder during viewing. Table 1 shows
the density of spores in solution that correspond to density of gametophytes
seen on the slides three days later.

Slides used for laboratory controls were kept in the settling trays. The
water was changed every 1-3 days. No special temperature or light regime.
was used: cultures were subjected to normal day-light cycles and ambient
temperatures of about 60°F. No extra nutrients were added, on the
assumption that the filtered sea water from Monterey Bay was rich enough.
in nutrients not to limit development.
