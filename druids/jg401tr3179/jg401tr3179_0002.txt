et al. 1984) Some parameters needed for success have been determined.
Deysher and Dean (1986) define a "recruitment window" for natural
production of sporophytes as temperature below 16.3°C and irradiation
levels above 0.4 E m-2 day-1. Similarly, North (1974) refers to an
unquantified "environmental turnstile" between spore release and
appearance of juvenile sporophytes. Devinny and Volse (1978) have found
that 10 mg cm-1 of sediment causes significant mortality to gametophytes,
and Luning and Neushul (1978) have determined critical light levels for
gametophytic reproductive success. Grazers such as sea urchins (see Ebeling
et al. 1985) and fish (Neushul and Haxo 1983) have been found to cause
mortality to adult and juvenile sporophytes, and some researchers have
recently begun to attempt to determine the effects of grazers on the
gametophytic stage as well. (L. Holts, pers. comm., D. Antonio, pers. comm.)
