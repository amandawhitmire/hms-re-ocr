Lab experiments

Results from the lab experiments show that grazing by Tegula does
cause mortality to Macrocystis gametophytes under controlled conditions.
Mortality on control slides was minimal during the course of the
experiments, while over 90% of gametophytes exposed to snail grazers
disappeared within 24 hours. This brought overall gametophyte density
down from about 180 gametophytes mm-2 to less than 10 mm-2. (figure 3)
Tegula exhibit typical grazing behavior (Hickman and Morris, 1985) when.
exposed to gametophytes on a frosted glass slide. They scrape the slide with
their radulae in a semi-circular pattern, leaving characteristic trails in the
gametophyte lawn. These trails were observed on all slides exposed to
Tegula in the lab.

Sea table experiments

Sea table experiments also showed effects of Tegula in Tegula -
accessible areas. Mortality among all gametophytes on all treatments,
however, was higher than in the lab. After 3 days in the sea table, 63%
mortality was observed on the copper-painted stones, compared to 95%
(about 3 gametophytes mm-2) on the untreated stones (figure 4). Due to
lack of time and sea-table space, this experiment was not replicated.
Statistical analysis was therefore not performed.
