The second set of field experiments was carried out a week later at 2.4
m. Only the full copper-treated and the untreated patio stones were used
this time, replicated 3 times. (Sites 4,5, and 6) All replicates were again.
within 2.5 m or less of an adult Macrocystis, and within 13 m of one
another. 8 gametophyte slides (density: 60.3 gametophytes mm-2) were
placed on each patio stone. Two slides from each stone were collected after
14 hours, 2 days, 5 days, and 7 days. No recruitment control slides were
used .

As an additional control during each field experiment, 2 extra
gametophyte slides were carried out to the field, then carried back to the lab
and counted at the end of the dive. Transport controls never showed
additional mortality over slides remaining in lab. Other experimenters have
found similar results with transport controls (Deysher and Dean 1986, Reed.
in prep.)

lab experiments

The lab experiments were carried out in a small aquarium divided
into three equal parts. Water could pass from one section to the next, but
snails could not. 2 gametophyte slides were held in each section by surgical
tubing woven through a 7.6 cm X 15.2 cm X 0.6 cm sheet of Plexiglass. 5
Tegula brunnes were put in one section, 5 7 pulligo in another, and the
third was left free of snails as a control. The slides for the first experiment
were plated at a high density of 181 gametophytes mm-2. The snails were
removed after three days and remaining gametophytes were counted. The
second experiment used slides of gametophyte density of 32.8 mm-2.

Gametophyte densities were recorded after 4 hours, then again when snails.
were removed after 24 hours.
