The fact that C. scabra can resist dislodgement by L
gigantea may also be a factor in determining why it is the only
species of limpets found on the backs of L. gigantea. Due to its
homing behavior, it is one of the few species that can resist
bulldozing and exist within an area otherwise completely cleared
of limpets. Because this bulldozed area normally surrounds the
L. gigantea, the C. scabra may be the only species of limpets
close enough to scramble up on the L. gigantea's shell. In fact,
Wright (1977) has shown that not only are Collissella digitalis
unable to overcome the bulldozing force, but they also tend to
recognize and actively avoid L. gigantea grazing areas, which
might explain why this species, which is similar in size,
distribution, and grazing behaviors to C. scabra, is not found on
L. gigantea shells. The rigidity of C. scabra's homing behavior
(Jessee, 1968, Villee & Groody, 1940, Hewatt, 1940) may also
explain its seemingly unique ability to live on the confined area
cigantea shell; due to its repeated foraging exposure to
the same areas surrounding its homesite, the species may be able
to reforage areas more successfully than other species. In
