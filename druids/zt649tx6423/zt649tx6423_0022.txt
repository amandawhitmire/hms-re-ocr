Palmer (1990) implies, the shell structure as a factor in

Also, these spots of decreased shell thickness are usually

these weak spots.

CONCLUSIONS

1.) The spacial density of C. scabra is ten times less on L.
gigantea than in the surrounding area, possibly due to limited
foraging area on the L. gigantea.

2.) In areas of increased wave action the density of riders on
the L. gigantea increases, possibly due to corresponding increase
in rate of algal growth caused by increased ambient water flow.
3.) Rider C. scabra have a higher ratio of shell mass per body
mass than do nonriders, possibly due to either a.) a slower rate
of body growth necessitating that the shell be laid down thicker,
or b.) the body mass being limited by the smaller internal volume
available created by the shell being laid down thicker.

4.) L. gigantea appear unaffected by the rider C. scabra.
