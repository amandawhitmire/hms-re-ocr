Methods

Field Studies

All field observations and samples of Lottia gigantea and
Collisella scabra were conducted at Hopkins Marine Station,
Pacific Grove, CA. Sites #1 and #2 were on two sheer rock faces
at Cabrillo Point, directly facing the oncoming surf and site #3
was a wall near Bird Rock at an angle of approximately 45 degrees
to the oncoming surf. I chose these sites because of their
abundance of Lottia gigantea at the widest range of intertidal
heights to be found at the Station.

I observed the L. gigantea and C. scabra during 30 daytime
low tides and three high tides: 1.) a high high tide at 2:00 a.m.
2.) a low high tide at 6:00 a.m. (1/2 hour after dawn) and 3.) a
high high tide at 4:00 p.m. The high tide watches were done at
sites 1 & 2 for six hours, beginning three hours before high

tide .

I measured the lengths and widths of all the L. gigantea and
all of their C. scabra riders found at each of the three sites.

The pedal area of the limpets was then estimated to be (pi) x
(length/2) x (width/2).

The surface area of the shells could not be directly

measured in the field.

However, within each species the shapes

of the shells, including the height of the apex, remains constant
as a function of length and width.

Therefore, surface area of

the shells depends primarily on the length and width of the shell
and the equation for pedal area was thus used as an estimation of
relative surface area of the shells within each species.
