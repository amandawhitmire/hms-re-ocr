Introduction

In this study I examine the interactions between Collisella
scabra and Lottia gigantea in particular the association between
the subpopulation of C. scabra which live on the surface of the
L. gigantea shell (henceforth referred to as riders).

What are

the differences between this subpopulation and the general C
scabra population (henceforth referred to as nonriders) and,
second, what are the differences between L. gigantea with and
without these C. scabra riders on their shells?

The existence of riders on limpets shells is not an entirely
new phenomenon. A surprising number of limpets live on the

shells of other animals Species of acmaeids and patellids live
on the shell of conspecifics until they become too large, at
which time they migrate onto the surrounding rock (Branch, 1971).
Other species, such as Patelloida insignis and Collisella asmi
are found to home on a series of other shells, changing host
shells rapidly (Eikenberry & Wickizer, 1964, Creese, 1978).
However, C. scabra, are unique in that they ride on the back of a
species other than their own and in greater density and numbers

than other riders,
