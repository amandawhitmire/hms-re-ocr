Proximity to L. gigantea may explain why the density of C.
scabra per area of L. gigantea shell is independent of density in
the measured surrounding area. As only some C.. scabra can resist
bulldozing (Wright, 1977), the density of C. scabra immediately
surrounding the L. gigantea is a reflection of the number of C.

scabra that could resist bulldozing.

This number is not

necessarily a function of the total number of C. scabra in the
larger surrounding area and is also considerably less the C.
scabra density in this larger area. If further experiments show
that approximately one in ten C. scabra could resist bulldozing
and thus that the density of C scabra was on average 10 times
less in the bulldozed area than in the surrounding area, it could
explain why density per area of L. gigantea shell is on average
ten times less than in the larger surrounding area. Rider
density would then simply be a function of of C. scabra density
in the immediate surrounding grazing area rather than in the
larger ungrazed surrounding area.

L. gigantea shell than per surrounding area could be explained by
numerous other factors. Perhaps the low density per area of
shell is because: 1.) It is ten times more likely for a C.
scabra to forage on a rock than on a mobile shell (This is
