ABSTRACT

The subpopulation of Collisella scabra found on the
shells of Lottia gigantea show significant differences from the
general population of C. scabra: population density is ten times
less, shell/body weight ratios are higher indicating slower
growth patterns possibly due to limited food availability, and
density on the shell increases in areas of high wave action
possibly due to the faster rate of algal growth in these areas.
The limited foraging area on the L. gigantea shell may provide an
upper limit to the number of riders it can support, as riders
from crowded shells do not lower their metabolic rates.

The

presence of these riders on the L. gigantea removes the algal
coat otherwise found on their shells. This loss of algae, the
creation of the C. scabra's home scar, the added weight of the
riders, or the drag differences created by the riders cause no
apparent advantage or disadvantage to the L. gigantea.
these results are in no way comprehensive, they provide a
background knowledge for further studies of the species
