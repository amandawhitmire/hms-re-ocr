DISCUSSION :

From the above results it is apparent that over a course of 18 days, a majority
of Clinocottus analis individuals have a high fidelity to specific tidepools and
regularly return to them at low tide. From the results of the displacement studies,
observed fidelity to a tidepool was not due simply to the fact that those fish were
replaced there at low tide after capture; hence, the appearance of an individual twice
at a certain tidepool indicated that other factors affecting fidelity were involved. Upon
comparing these findings with those of Richkus (1978), I was surprised to discover
the remarkably small percentage of fish that missed more than 1 consecutive day
away from the original pool of capture, for he reported that samplings in two week
intervals showed a low percent of recapture of tagged individuals in their original
pools, and he further demonstrated a rise in this percent recapture as he sampled all
pools in the area. It is interesting to note that I found only 3 individuals out of 31 that
appeared in any one of the other 5 pools observed, comprising only 14% of the
population, as opposed to the 67% of tagged fish that appeared only in their original
pools and occasionally moved elsewhere out of the study site for no more than one
low tide. These findings suggest that the daily movements of individuals over an
approximately two-week period follows a markedly different pattern than those
reported for the same species in a different habitat, sampled biweekly. An alteration
of the earlier schematic due to these new findings suggests that, on the time scale
observed, the majority of individuals have either one or more pools to which they are
faithful outside of the study area, or they have a series of pools in which they reside
