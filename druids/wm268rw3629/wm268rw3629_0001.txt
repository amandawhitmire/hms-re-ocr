Abstract

Different methods of inducing spawning in the gastropod Acmea
incessa were investigated. No reliable method was found that could
be used as a generalized technique for mollusc spawning. To stain the
chromosomes of the eggs and developing zygotes, I used a method that
required preliminary fixation in carnoys solution followed by DAPI
stain. The developing stages of the zygote chromosomes immediately
after fertilization up until the first cleavage are documented.

Oogenesis, fertilization, and development of the embryo has
been studied extensively in echinoids (Strathmann, 1987). Part of
the reason these gametes have been used is the ease with which they
can be studied. There are standard methods for constantly inducing
spawning and large amounts of gametes can be obtained. Also, the
eggs are transparent enough to get a clear view of the internal
organization of the egg during the first stages of development.

Molluscan eggs have been studied little in the past because
working with the animals and the eggs is difficult. There are no
reliable methods for inducing spawning. Also, the eggs are opaque and
their contents are not easily distinguishable.
