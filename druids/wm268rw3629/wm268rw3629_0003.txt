In some cases, eggs were also acquired by disecting females in
ammonia sea water (sea water titrated to pH 9.5 with NH OH). The
ammonia sea water causes gernimal vesicle (GV) breakdown. While
holding the animal in the ammonia sea water, an incision was made in
the gonad and oocytes flushed out of the opening. The released
oocytes were allowed to settle to the bottom of the dish and the
excess sea water was removed by aspiration. The eggs were then
placed in fresh ammonia FSW and allowed to sit for one to two hours
until GV breakdown which is necessary before normal fertilization
can occur. After GV breakdown and before fertilizing, the eggs were
transferred to normal sea water.

Fixing and staining techniques:

Eggs suspended in FSW were centrifuged, the sea water
removed by aspiration, and the eggs resuspended in carnoys fixative
(3 ethanol: 1 acetic acid). After and hour of fixing in carnoys, the
eggs were washed in a 70% ethanol solution and recentrifuged. The
ethanol was removed by aspiration and the remaining traces were
allowed to evaporate. The eggs were resuspended in FSW and
4,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI) stain was added to make a final
concentration of Sug/ml (Linda Goff, 1988).DAPI (Sigma Corporation)
was in a stock solution of 1mg/ml in dimethylsulfoxide( DMSO). After
fifteen minutes of staining, the eggs were viewed under a
fluorescence microscope with a UV filter to determine the
cytological state of the chromosomes.
